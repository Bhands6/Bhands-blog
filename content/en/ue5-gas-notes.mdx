I recently migrated a small UE5 project from hand-rolled skill logic to GAS (GameplayAbilitySystem). There is plenty of material out there, but the real pitfalls only show up once you start. This is my notes-to-self so the next person saves a few hours.

## Nail down the three core concepts first

GAS has three building blocks, and they are easy to confuse at the start:

- **AbilitySystemComponent (ASC)**: the entry point, attached to an Actor. Everything flows through it.
- **GameplayAbility (GA)**: a skill itself — dash, fireball — owning logic and flow control.
- **GameplayEffect (GE)**: the data carrier. Attribute changes, buffs, damage — all GEs.

One line to remember: **GA decides what to do, GE decides what to change, ASC makes the match.**

## Pitfall 1: forgot to register the AttributeSet

I added a custom `UMyAttributeSet` to the character but health values never behaved. After way too long I found I never handed the set over to the ASC:

```cpp
AbilitySystemComponent = CreateDefaultSubobject<UMyAbilitySystemComponent>(TEXT("AbilitySystem"));
AbilitySystemComponent->SetIsReplicated(true);
// Critical: hand the AttributeSet to the ASC, otherwise nothing works
AbilitySystemComponent->InitAbilityActorInfo(this, this);
MyAttributeSet = CreateDefaultSubobject<UMyAttributeSet>(TEXT("AttributeSet"));
```

> Advice: keep all ASC initialization in one function. If it is scattered, you will eventually miss a step.

## Pitfall 2: polling instead of GameplayTags

Checking "is the character stunned" via Tick polling is a trap. The right way is event-driven:

1. The stun GE applies the `State.Stunned` tag;
2. GAs listen for tag add and remove events;
3. When the tag appears, cancel movement-related GAs automatically.

No polling anywhere, and both performance and readability win.

## Wrapping up

GAS has a steep curve, but it decomposes "skills" cleanly. After these pitfalls my verdict flipped from "why is this so complicated" to "things should have always been built this way". Next post will cover GE and GameplayCue working together.
